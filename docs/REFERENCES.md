# References

These sources ground the project scope and the standards-level terminology. Verify the relevant
edition and the exact claim before citing it in the final report.

1. C. H. Bennett and G. Brassard, “Quantum cryptography: Public key distribution and coin
   tossing,” *Proceedings of IEEE International Conference on Computers, Systems and Signal
   Processing*, 1984, pp. 175–179. Foundational BB84 protocol reference.
2. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), *Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation
   Mechanism Standard*, FIPS 203, August 2024. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.FIPS.203>.
   Normative ML-KEM algorithm specification.
3. NIST, *Recommendation for Key Management: Part 1 – General*, SP 800-57 Part 1 Revision 5,
   May 2020. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-57pt1r5>. General key-management context;
   consult current applicable guidance.
4. European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI), *Quantum Key Distribution (QKD);
   Use Cases*, ETSI GS QKD 002 V1.1.1, June 2010. ETSI standards search:
   <https://www.etsi.org/standards-search>.
5. ETSI, *Quantum Key Distribution (QKD); Protocol and data format of REST-based key delivery
   API*, ETSI GS QKD 014 V1.1.1, February 2019. ETSI standards search:
   <https://www.etsi.org/standards-search>.
6. P. W. Shor, “Algorithms for quantum computation: discrete logarithms and factoring,”
   *Proceedings of the 35th Annual Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science*, 1994,
   pp. 124–134. DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1994.365700>. Context for quantum threats to
   some classical public-key assumptions.

## Evidence discipline

Standards specify algorithms or interfaces; they do not establish performance on this project's
machine. The BB84 reference does not validate the simplified simulator as a secure implementation.
Any deployment comparison requires additional current primary sources and clear evidence labels.
